# WhatsApp and Facebook Groups as Course / Community Tools (Egypt and MENA focus)

Research date: 2026-10-20. About 18 tool calls. The official WhatsApp Help Center pages could not be read (the fetch returned a truncated page), so some feature limits come from third-party sources. These are flagged.

## 1. Current WhatsApp features relevant to education (2025–2026)

### Takeaway
WhatsApp groups now have real moderation and onboarding tools: admin approval, history sharing with new members, member tags and events. They are still a chat stream. There are no threaded topics, and the history is not institutional. Usernames that hide phone numbers were announced in June 2026. Only reservations are open, and the feature rolls out by country "later this year". As of October 2026, do not assume that it hides numbers in Egyptian groups.

### Cited Findings
- **Group size:** WhatsApp raised the maximum number of participants per group to 1,024 (from 512) when it launched Communities in Nov 2022. Group calls are capped at 32 participants. — [GSMArena](https://m.gsmarena.com/whatsapp_unveils_communities_feature_raises_group_limit_to_1024_participants-news-56392.php); [Kanal guide](https://getkanal.com/blog/community-guide-whatsapp)
- **Communities, limits (not confirmed from an official source; sources conflict):**
  - A 2026 third-party guide says a Community holds up to 50 groups, including the announcement group, and up to 5,000 members. — [Kanal 2026 guide](https://getkanal.com/blog/community-guide-whatsapp); [Telecrm](https://telecrm.in/blog/whatsapp-community/)
  - Another blog, citing the Help Center, says 2,000 members. An API help desk also gives 2,000 for the community plus its announcement group. — [timelines.ai](https://timelines.ai/increase-whatsapp-group-members-limit); [Whapi](https://support.whapi.cloud/help-desk/communities)
  - At launch in 2022 the limit was 10 groups per community. — [The Star (Kenya), 2022](https://www.the-star.co.ke/news/2022-11-18-how-to-use-whatsapp-communities)
- **Announcement group:** WhatsApp creates it automatically. Only admins can post, and every community member can read it. — [Kanal](https://getkanal.com/blog/community-guide-whatsapp)
- **Admin approval of joins:** Admins can require approval for people who join through an invite link or a community, and they see all requests in one place (2023). — [TechCrunch, 21 Mar 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/03/21/whatsapps-new-feature-gives-admins-an-easier-way-to-control-who-can-join-a-group); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/whatsapp-group-admins-can-approve-who-joins-communities-115003246.html)
- **Chat history for new members (Group Message History):** The person who adds a new member can choose to send 25 to 100 recent messages. The history stays end-to-end encrypted, all members get a notice, and admins can turn the feature off. If nobody shares history, the new member sees nothing that came before. — [GSMArena](https://www.gsmarena.com/whatsapp_introduces_new_group_message_history_feature-news-71641.php); [Thurrott](https://www.thurrott.com/cloud/332894/whatsapp-now-lets-group-admins-share-message-history-with-newcomers); [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/how-it-works/whatsapps-group-message-history-feature-explained-how-new-members-can-now-catch-up-on-chats-90247.htm). I could not confirm whether this works inside Community groups.
- **Member tags, event reminders (Jan 2026):** Members can add a short tag that says who they are or what role they have in a group. Sources disagree on whether admins or members control the tags. Group events can have custom early reminders. — [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-whatsapp-member-tags/); [AlternativeTo, Jan 2026](https://alternativeto.net/news/2026/1/whatsapp-rolls-out-new-group-chat-features-member-tags-text-stickers-and-event-reminders/); [WABetaInfo](https://wabetainfo.com/whatsapp-is-rolling-out-member-tags-for-group-chats-text-stickers-and-event-reminders/)
- **Usernames and phone-number privacy (announced, NOT fully launched):**
  - On 29 June 2026 WhatsApp announced usernames: "Starting this week, you can reserve a username to use later this year when we launch this feature." The rollout is gradual, and users get a notice when it reaches their country. No country list is public. — [BleepingComputer](https://bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/whatsapp-rolls-out-usernames-to-help-users-hide-their-phone-number)
  - When the feature is active, people who do not have your number in their contacts see your username instead. AFP reports that your number is not shared automatically when you join a large group. Smartprix reports that group members see the username in place of the number. — [Arab News/AFP](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2649006); [Smartprix](https://www.smartprix.com/bytes/whatsapp-usernames-are-here-you-can-now-chat-without-sharing-your-phone-number/)
  - An optional "username key" (Smartprix says a 4-digit code) must be known before someone can message you. There is no username directory or search. Some usernames are reserved for governments, public figures and businesses. — [BleepingComputer](https://bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/whatsapp-rolls-out-usernames-to-help-users-hide-their-phone-number); [Smartprix](https://www.smartprix.com/bytes/whatsapp-usernames-are-here-you-can-now-chat-without-sharing-your-phone-number/)
  - Limits: you still need a phone number to sign up, and WhatsApp still collects metadata about who messages whom and when. Carissa Véliz (quoted by the BBC) says WhatsApp is not privacy-friendly overall. — [Smartprix](https://www.smartprix.com/bytes/whatsapp-usernames-are-here-you-can-now-chat-without-sharing-your-phone-number/)
  - Timing conflict: Smartprix says a phased rollout over a few months. GadgetReview says a broader launch later in 2026, and the early rollout reaches only some users. — [GadgetReview](https://www.gadgetreview.com/?p=389724)

### Inferences
- Before usernames reach Egypt, every member of a WhatsApp course group can see every other member's phone number. A phone number in Egypt is linked to a national ID through SIM registration (I have no source for this in these notes; verify it). After usernames arrive, the protection depends on each user turning it on. It is not the default for the group.
- WhatsApp's history-sharing feature is opt-in and capped at 100 messages, so it confirms the "lost history" problem. Late joiners and the institution do not get the archive automatically.
- Communities give a three-level structure that resembles CILAS's plan: community-wide announcement group, programme community, course sub-groups. But the sub-groups are flat chat, and the limits are not verified.

### Gaps
- I could not read the official Help Center figures for Communities (groups per community, member cap, announcement-group cap). The fetch was truncated. Verify these at faq.whatsapp.com before quoting them.
- Not researched in detail: polls (single and multiple choice exist; WhatsApp added polls in 2022, but I did not confirm current details), reactions, "report to admin", in-group search, data export (Export chat), multi-device and desktop limits, Channels. These are well-known features, but I have no source in these notes.
- I found no source on the quality of Arabic/RTL support in WhatsApp or Facebook Groups. Both are widely used in Arabic, but there are no formal evaluations.

## 2. Facebook Groups features for education

### Takeaway
Facebook Groups has strong admin tooling: Admin Home, Admin Assist auto-moderation, membership questions and criteria, slow mode, member summaries. I could not confirm the current status of the "Social Learning" group type with Units. Facebook has removed several group features since 2021 (Rooms, Group Stories, the standalone Groups and Campus products), so availability is uncertain.

### Cited Findings
- **Social Learning groups / Units:** A Social Media Examiner guide describes "Social Learning" as a group type that unlocks Units for course content. That is a how-to guide, and I found no current confirmation that the feature is still available, nor any report that Facebook removed it. — [Social Media Examiner](https://www.socialmediaexaminer.com/?p=150982); 2019 test notes say Units were hard to reach on mobile web: [GitHub EticaAI](https://github.com/EticaAI/forum/issues/67)
- **Removed group features:** Group Rooms were removed, Group Stories were ended, and Facebook Campus (the college-only network) shut down in 2022. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-announces-removal-rooms-facebook-groups/697990/); [Ubergizmo, Mar 2022](https://www.ubergizmo.com/2022/03/facebook-campus-shut-down/); [Presse-citron](https://www.presse-citron.net/facebook-annonce-la-fin-des-stories-de-groupe/)
- **Admin tools (mostly announced in 2021):** Admin Home dashboard. Admin Assist with admin-set criteria to moderate posts and comments, such as auto-declining comments with outside links or blocking new accounts or new members. Slow mode, member summaries, and automatic approve or decline of join requests based on criteria such as answering the membership questions. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/facebook-adds-a-range-of-new-group-admin-tools-including-an-updated-dashbo/601941); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/news/facebook-unveils-new-group-admin-tools-moderation-can-be-automated-now); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2282039)
- **Young people leaving Facebook (US data):** In Pew's 2024 survey, 32% of US teens use Facebook, down from 71% in 2014–15 (roughly stable in recent years). About 90% use YouTube. — [Pew 2024 report PDF](https://pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2024/12/PI_2024.12.12_Teens-Social-Media-Tech_REPORT.pdf); [Pew Dec 2025 report](https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2025/12/PI_2025.12.09_Teens-Social-Media-AI_REPORT.pdf)
- **Arab youth:** One Arab Youth Survey write-up gives regional daily Facebook use falling from 85% to 72% over about two years, with WhatsApp first at 82% daily use. In Saudi Arabia daily Facebook use fell from 82% to 55%. TikTok daily use rose from 21% to 50%. In the 2023 survey (15th edition), the "preferred channel" figures were Facebook 18%, Instagram 17%, WhatsApp 16%, YouTube 13%, TikTok 12%. — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2167336); [Consultancy-me](https://www.consultancy-me.com/news/7110/arab-youths-addicted-to-social-media-face-mental-wellbeing-backlash). The years of the survey waves are not consistent across write-ups.

### Inferences
- In MENA, Facebook is not "dead" for young adults. Egypt's Facebook ad reach still grew 10.6% year on year (see section 5). But it is no longer the default space for students, and the regional trend is towards WhatsApp, Instagram and TikTok.
- Facebook Groups gives better structure than WhatsApp (posts and comments, search, files, admin automation), but it puts course discussion next to the student's social identity and feed.

### Gaps
- Anonymous posting in groups, Guides, and the 2025–2026 status of Social Learning Units: I found no current, reliable source. Check Meta's Help Center.

## 3. Peer-reviewed studies on WhatsApp in (higher) education, including MENA

### Takeaway
The evidence is consistent. WhatsApp wins on adoption, immediacy, cost and simplicity, and it builds belonging and peer help. It costs instructors in message overload, expected round-the-clock availability, exposure to students' personal lives, informal language and off-topic noise. MENA studies (mostly Saudi, some Egyptian) report positive student attitudes, but most of the use is for announcements and questions, not deep learning. Most samples are small and come from one institution.

### Cited Findings
- **Bouhnik & Deshen (2014), "WhatsApp Goes to School", JITE: Research 13, pp. 217–231.** Qualitative interviews with 12 teachers (Israel) who ran class WhatsApp groups. — [JITE PDF](https://www.jite.org/documents/Vol13/JITEv13ResearchP217-231Bouhnik0601.pdf)
  - Purposes: communicating with students, fostering a positive social atmosphere and belonging, peer dialogue, and a learning platform.
  - Technical advantages: simplicity, free of charge, availability and immediacy, "privacy & minimal exposure", students and teachers already use it. Teachers rated it better than email (students do not check email), SMS and Facebook groups.
  - The three teachers who had run Facebook class groups said they had "more drawbacks than benefits". One said a Facebook group "wasn't as prompt", and that students "feel quite exposed since the grownups joined". Another gave up because students from other classes sent friend requests, and girls felt offended when he did not accept.
  - Educational and academic advantages: teachers got to know students better, students helped one another (often a student answered before the teacher), shy students could see others' questions, groups continued after the course as alumni groups, and teachers could correct mistakes at once.
  - Challenges: students with no smartphone; "message flooding", which was time-consuming, especially with several groups or groups of more than 15 students; late-hour messages; group maintenance; expectation that teachers are always available; students' inappropriate language; overexposure to students' personal lives; informal or incorrect language; students expect a "here and now" solution and do not make an effort. Coping strategies: mute notifications, choose when to answer, remove disruptive members.
- **Israeli follow-ups:** high-school students resisted teachers' demands more on WhatsApp than in class (ignoring teachers, cynical messages). Staff saw WhatsApp as positive, but it was also used to humiliate or ostracise a pupil. — [Open University of Israel, Chais 2019](https://www.openu.ac.il/innovation/chais2019/f/a1_4.pdf); [Chais 2021](https://www.openu.ac.il/Lists/MediaServer_Documents/innovation/chais/2021/e3_17.pdf)
- **Saudi Arabia:**
  - Qassim University survey of about 200 students and staff (IJACSA vol. 10 no. 8, 2019; co-author from Damietta University, Egypt): heavy use of WhatsApp for educational support, preferred over the university's own platforms. — [SAI PDF](https://ftp.saiconference.com/Downloads/Volume10No8/Paper_53-WhatsApp_as_an_Educational_Support_Tool.pdf)
  - King Abdulaziz University, 2019 (AWEJ, EFL): students view WhatsApp favourably, but use it mostly for announcements and inquiries, not language learning. — [AWEJ](https://awej.org/students-opinions-on-the-functions-and-usefulness-of-communication-on-whatsapp-in-the-efl-higher-education-context/)
  - University of Tabuk, 2023 (World Journal of English Language 13(3)), n=100 EFL undergraduates: WhatsApp activities had a positive effect on perceptions of learning. — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/jfr/wjel11/v13y2023i3p104.html)
  - Saudi undergraduates' perceptions study. — [Saudi Digital Library](https://drepo.sdl.edu.sa/items/f4ed13ab-9842-4254-8667-e8a4e18a221a/full)
- **Jordan:** Gasaymeh (2017), "University Students' use of WhatsApp and their Perceptions Regarding its Possible Integration into their Education". I saw only the bibliographic record and have no findings. — [CORE](https://core.ac.uk/works/68997686)
- **Egypt:**
  - A survey of 400 randomly selected students at Ain Shams, Cairo and Helwan universities (Egyptian journal, partly in Arabic) found positive attitudes towards WhatsApp for the educational process. — [EKB journal PDF](https://molag.journals.ekb.eg/article_200414_622c8a4f52426546597475ed863394a2.pdf)
  - Ain Shams University, 275 mass-communication education students: mixed results. Frequency of use had no significant effect on academic purposes, but rate of usage did. — [ASU repository](https://research.asu.edu.eg/handle/123456789/179267)
- **Other:** first-year university students use WhatsApp daily for personal and social purposes, but their educational use is limited. Distraction is a reported drawback when students do not manage their time. — [Midlands State Univ. (Zimbabwe) PDF](https://cris.library.msu.ac.zw/jspui/bitstream/11408/5949/1/Whats%20up%20with%20the%20WhatsApp%20application%20in%20the%20classroom.pdf); [Atlantis Press](https://www.atlantis-press.com/article/126025365.pdf)

### Inferences
- The benefits of WhatsApp come mainly from the fact that people already use it ("students already use it"). A custom in-app groups feature must overcome that cost of change. The documented harms (overload, availability pressure, exposure, lost history) are what a designed tool can address: threads, quiet hours, institutional archive, no phone numbers.
- The 2014 teachers rated WhatsApp above Facebook for privacy. That is relative privacy: hidden from a social profile and feed, but phone numbers visible to the group.

### Gaps
- I found no large peer-reviewed study that specifically measures harassment or phone-number misuse in university WhatsApp groups in MENA.
- I found no study that ranks the platforms Egyptian students prefer for study groups. The Egyptian studies above measure attitudes, not platform rankings.

## 4. Studies on Facebook Groups in higher education

### Takeaway
Students value private Facebook study groups for peer support and feedback. They resist formal, instructor-led academic use of their social platform and want to keep coursework and instructor contact in separate private tools. Role confusion and boundary blurring come up often.

### Cited Findings
- Dennen & Burner (2013, Florida State University; AoIR Selected Papers of Internet Research): students share freely in social settings but are hesitant to contribute in academic settings. They do not want social media in coursework and prefer private tools for coursework and for talking with instructors. What students share with social friends may not be what they want to share with classmates or instructors. — [SPIR article](https://spir.aoir.org/ojs/index.php/spir/article/view/8480); [SPIR related paper](https://spir.aoir.org/ojs/index.php/spir/article/download/8593/6842)
- University of Greenwich EdD thesis (22 students): private Facebook study groups can provide peer feedback and social support, but Facebook has limited value for front-of-classroom teaching. The nature of the shared object influenced sharing decisions more than the network. — [GALA Greenwich](https://gala.gre.ac.uk/id/eprint/22918)
- The edited volume *An Education in Facebook? Higher Education and the World's Largest Social Network* (Routledge) has chapters on Facebook Groups for first-year support, "Role Confusion in Facebook Groups", and a section on "Boundaries and Privacy". — [Routledge](https://www.routledge.com/products/9780415713191)
- Teachers in Bouhnik & Deshen (2014) left Facebook class groups over friend-request pressure, slowness and students' sense of exposure. — [JITE PDF](https://www.jite.org/documents/Vol13/JITEv13ResearchP217-231Bouhnik0601.pdf)

### Inferences
- The boundary problem supports a separate, institution-run space where academic identity is distinct from social identity. This is an argument for an in-app feature.

### Gaps
- I found no MENA-specific peer-reviewed study on Facebook Groups in higher education in this pass.

### Takeaway
Egypt has 98.2M internet users (82.7%). Facebook is the largest measured social platform (51.6M ad reach, still growing). TikTok and YouTube are close behind. DataReportal publishes no WhatsApp figure for Egypt, because Meta does not publish WhatsApp ad reach. Regional youth surveys put WhatsApp at or near the top for daily use.

- Internet users: 98.2M (82.7%), up 2.4% year on year. 20.6M people (17.3%) offline. Mobile connections: 121M (102% of population).
- Social media user identities: 51.6M (43.4% of population). 18+: 51.3M (68.8% of adults). Gender split 39.0% female, 61.0% male.
- Ad reach: Facebook 51.6M (52.5% of internet users; +10.6% Oct 2024–Oct 2025; 68.8% of adults). YouTube 49.3M (-2.8%). TikTok 48.8M (18+ only; 65.5% of adults; +25.2%). Messenger 37.0M. Instagram 21.7M (+13.9%). Snapchat 20.6M. LinkedIn 15.0M members. X 4.64M (-15.4%). Threads 3.15M. Reddit 1.35M.

### Inferences

- The lack of a WhatsApp figure is a measurement gap, not low use.

### Gaps

## 6. Privacy, data ownership and legal points

### Takeaway
Egypt's PDPL (Law 151/2020) became operational when its Executive Regulations were issued on 1 Nov 2025 (Ministerial Decree 816/2025). The compliance grace period ends around 1 Nov 2026, or possibly the end of 2026. This affects CILAS directly: licensing, a DPO, consent in Arabic, cross-border transfer permits, retention periods and 72-hour breach notice. Running groups on WhatsApp or Facebook does not remove CILAS's own duties, and it gives CILAS no control over retention or export. In Egypt the political risk of exposing group membership is real.

### Cited Findings
- **Executive Regulations:** issued 1 Nov 2025 under Ministerial Decree 816/2025. Made public around 25 Dec 2025. Strict reading: enforcement from 1 Nov 2026, but the regulator may treat the grace period as running to the end of 2026. The Personal Data Protection Centre (PDPC) is the regulator and has issued guidance on consent and DPOs. — [CMS](https://cms.law/en/are/legal-updates/egypt-s-pdpl-executive-regulations-issued-one-year-compliance-countdown-begins); [Al Tamimi](https://www.tamimi.com/law_update_articles/from-policy-to-practice-egypt-issues-executive-regulations-of-the-personal-data-protection-law/); [Clyde & Co, Jan 2026](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2026/01/egypt-regulatory-update-on-data-privacy); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/privacy-protection/1768986/the-issuance-of-the-executive-regulations-of-the-data-protection-law-in-egypt-and-the-establishment-of-the-data-protection-centre)
- **Key obligations (CMS summary):** consent must be explicit, informed, specific and freely given, with records kept. Consent is not suitable where there is a power imbalance or where a service depends on it, which matters for student relationships. Written consent for sensitive data. Parental consent for children's data, in Arabic. Cross-border transfers, including cloud hosting, need a licence or permit and possibly a Transfer Impact Assessment. Controllers and processors need a general licence (about 3 years; 90 working days to review). Foreign entities need a local representative. A registered DPO is required. Notify the PDPC of a breach within 72 hours and affected people within 3 days of that. Defined retention periods, a register of processing activities (ROPA), and privacy notices in Arabic. — [CMS](https://cms.law/en/are/legal-updates/egypt-s-pdpl-executive-regulations-issued-one-year-compliance-countdown-begins)
- **Safety context, Egypt:** In 2026 Amnesty documented dozens of people detained over the "Gen Z002" online group, among them school and university students aged 19 to 46. Six men were subjected to enforced disappearance for 6 to 40 days and were then questioned about when and how they joined the group (Discord, not WhatsApp). The admins used pseudonymous accounts. — [Amnesty International, July 2026](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2026/07/egypt-release-dozens-detained-over-gen-z-online-group-calling-for-political-change/)
- In 2014 Egyptian students were arrested over Facebook pages they ran, and police seized phones and laptops. — [Ahram Online](https://english.ahram.org.eg/News/114239.aspx)
- **WhatsApp metadata:** even with usernames, WhatsApp collects metadata about who messages whom and when. — [Smartprix (citing BBC)](https://www.smartprix.com/bytes/whatsapp-usernames-are-here-you-can-now-chat-without-sharing-your-phone-number/)

### Inferences
- In politically sensitive contexts, group membership itself is evidence: who joined, when, and phone numbers. On WhatsApp the membership list and numbers sit on every member's phone. Message content is end-to-end encrypted, but a seized, unlocked phone shows everything. An in-app feature concentrates the data at CILAS. That gives CILAS control (retention, deletion, pseudonyms) and also responsibility (a target for legal requests, breach duties under PDPL). Neither option is "safe" by default.
- Under PDPL, the institution's own processing (rosters, announcements) still needs a lawful basis. CILAS cannot set retention or export policy for WhatsApp or Facebook data.

### Gaps
- GDPR applicability for EU-resident fellows, and Meta's data-sharing practices between WhatsApp and Facebook (including EU vs. non-EU differences): not researched in this pass.
- I found no confirmed Egyptian case where WhatsApp group membership or phone numbers were the main evidence. The Discord case is the closest recent example.

## 7. Known patterns: LMS/forum combined with WhatsApp

### Takeaway
The literature recommends a blend. Students prefer WhatsApp for quick coordination and peer support. The LMS or forum keeps structure and a durable record. Few authors suggest that WhatsApp replace the forum.

### Cited Findings
- Mpungose (2020), South Africa: first-year students who had to use Moodle said they would prefer WhatsApp. The author recommends blending formal and informal platforms. — [TPACK refs](https://tpack.pages.wm.edu/archives/129)
- UNISA chapter: students prefer WhatsApp for ownership, immediacy and cost. The authors say WhatsApp is limited for facilitated discussion and is not a replacement for a threaded LMS forum. — [UNISA IR](https://ir.unisa.ac.za/handle/10500/23493?show=full)
- Universitas Terbuka (IATED 2026): a two-way sync of WhatsApp group messages with the LMS forum increased communication, sped up coordination, and helped tutors monitor participation. — [IATED](https://library.iated.org/view/JULAEHA2026LEV)
- Mataram University (Indonesia, ICESS 2024), n=27: some students preferred WhatsApp for short-term talk, others preferred the LMS features. The authors recommend integration. — [Unram](https://conference.unram.ac.id/event/4/contributions/105/contribution.pdf)
- Telegram groups vs. Moodle forums (University of the Basque Country): Telegram was more dynamic, with higher engagement among students who joined. — [EHU](https://ekoizpen-zientifikoa.ehu.eus/documentos/60cd62c063d7a5247e8da2f7)
- Vendor claim (not peer-reviewed): a Moodle–WhatsApp integration gave 95% message delivery and +40% engagement. — [Edwiser](https://edwiser.org/edwiser-customer-success-stories/how-team-green-world-group-increased-message-delivery-by-95-and-boosted-student-engagement-by-40-with-moodle-whatsapp-integration/)

### Inferences
- A hybrid fits CILAS: an in-app space for discussion, polls, archive and moderation, with WhatsApp (Channel, or a Community announcement group) or push notifications only for alerts that link back into the app. This answers the strongest pro-WhatsApp evidence (immediacy, reach) without moving the record onto members' phones.

### Gaps
- I found no documented MENA university that formally uses the "WhatsApp for alerts, forum for discussion" pattern, though it is likely common informally.
